# LumiRender literature review and design decisions

## Search protocol

The reproducible screen is generated by `python -m daynight.literature_screen`. Five OpenAlex queries cover day/night translation, relighting/inverse rendering, intrinsic decomposition, night camera effects, and structure-preserving unpaired translation. The current deduplicated artifact contains **245 records**, satisfying the planned 200-abstract minimum: [LUMIRENDER_LITERATURE_SCREEN.csv](LUMIRENDER_LITERATURE_SCREEN.csv).

Records are ranked by relevance and citation count, then retained when their title or abstract contains a mechanism relevant to the project. The table below is the 30-source primary-paper implementation review. “Adopt” means locally reimplemented or used as a training/evaluation principle; “teacher/reference” means external work is attributed and is not claimed as the architecture.

## Adopted synthesis

No reviewed method alone meets the project constraints. LumiRender combines four evidence-backed layers: intrinsic scene factorization, spatially constrained stochastic lighting, image-space material/reflection rendering, and a camera model. The correction network is intentionally weaker than the renderer. This is the main architectural claim.

## Rejected directions

- **3D Gaussian splatting, NeRF and full inverse path tracing:** need multi-view observations or known geometry and exceed the single-image problem definition.
- **A larger CycleGAN/CUT variant:** may improve texture metrics but does not make emitters, exposure or reflections identifiable.
- **Unrestricted diffusion editing:** is a strong external reference but risks invented geometry and obscures which part is the team's work.
- **Pure Retinex inversion:** factorizes luminance but does not decide where nighttime artificial lights and reflections belong.
- **V2.1-style strong high-frequency preservation:** its failed ablation showed that soft structure objectives can suppress the required lighting change.

This review is an engineering evidence matrix, not a claim that LumiRender has already achieved state-of-the-art results. Acceptance remains empirical.
